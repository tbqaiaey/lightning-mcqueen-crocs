## The three Lightning McQueen Crocs, sorted out

The original Cars x Crocs Classic Clog "Lightning McQueen" came out in kids' sizing back in 2017. Adult sizes didn't arrive until 2019, which is when things got strange — Men's Journal reported people queueing at stores and resellers flipping pairs for several times retail. That pair is the red one with the LED lights in the sole that flash as you walk, style code **205759-610**, and a retail price of **$70** at last listing.

The 2025 Cars collection (dropped May 20 that year) brought the red light-up McQueen back alongside a Mater clog, with the range running from about $5 for Jibbitz charms up to $70 for the clogs.

The newest pair is different in one big way: it isn't red. The **Disney and Pixar's Lightning McQueen Dinoco Crocband Clog** puts McQueen in the blue-and-orange Dinoco livery he daydreamed about in the first film, and it tacks an actual wing onto the heel strap. Style code **213582-90H**, released **September 16, 2026**, as part of the Cars franchise's 20th anniversary.

## Sizing is where most of the complaints land

Browse reviews on Crocs' own product pages and at third-party sellers and one theme repeats: they run small. Buyers describe the toe pressing against the front with the strap engaged, or needing to size up from their usual Crocs size — one reviewer who normally wears an 8 in every other Crocs style found these too tight in an 8.

The second recurring complaint is about the lights on the original red version. Multiple buyers report one shoe's LED dying within days or months, and at least one notes there's no off switch and no way to replace the battery once it goes. It's a novelty feature on a shoe made of foam, so that's a design limitation rather than a defect you can fix.

Third: returns. Several reviewers on Crocs' regional pages note they couldn't return a pair that fit wrong. Worth checking the return terms for the country you're buying in before choosing a size in a hurry.

## Where pairs actually turn up

Crocs sells the Dinoco clog directly online and in its own retail stores, with "select retailers" also carrying it. The Disney Store has listed the pair too, at $74.99. Reports from the launch put physical availability in the US, Australia and Malaysia, with other markets likely to follow the pattern of previous Cars drops.

Stock behaviour is the part worth planning around. The red McQueen clog has been restocked almost every year since 2019 and has reportedly sold out on each of those runs. The Dinoco version is a 20th-anniversary release tied to a specific movie moment, which is exactly the kind of thing that doesn't sit on shelves for a year. If you want a pair at retail rather than resale, waiting for a discount is a gamble — there's no history of these being discounted after launch, because they don't tend to last.

Resale exists, and it's colder than it was. The early flipping frenzy described in 2019 cooled off in later years, though pairs still trade above retail on sneaker platforms when a release sells through.
